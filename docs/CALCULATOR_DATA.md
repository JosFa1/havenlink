# Financial Calculator — data provenance and verification

The calculator's engine is deterministic and unit-tested. **Its numbers are research.**
This document records where every seeded figure came from and what still needs a human
to sign it off.

Authoring instructions (adding a state, rolling a tax year) live next to the data, in
[`src/data/calculator/README.md`](../src/data/calculator/README.md).

---

## Standing requirement

> **Every row in `src/data/calculator/` must be verified by a person against its
> `source_url` before these figures are relied on in production.**

They were read from primary government sources at authoring time and transcribed
carefully, but transcription is not verification. Two things make this a recurring
job rather than a one-off:

- **Tax figures change every year.** The 2026 credit, phase-out, brackets, and standard
  deductions are all superseded the moment the IRS publishes the 2027 revenue procedure.
- **State fee schedules change without notice** and rarely announce it.

A row whose source URL 404s, or whose figure no longer matches the page, should be
**deleted rather than guessed at**. A deleted row falls back to the national range and
shows the family an honest banner. A stale row shows them a confident wrong number.

## Sources, as seeded

### Federal tax constants — tax year 2026

Source: **IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32**, <https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf>

| Figure | Value | Where in the document |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum adoption credit | $17,670 | §4.04(2) |
| Refundable portion | $5,120 | §4.04(3) |
| MAGI phase-out begins | $265,080 | §4.04(2) |
| MAGI phase-out complete | $305,080 | §4.04(2) |
| Special-needs credit | $17,670 (full, regardless of expenses) | §4.04(1) |
| Standard deductions | $16,100 single / $32,200 MFJ / $16,100 MFS / $24,150 HoH | §4.14(1) |
| Rate schedules | All four filing statuses | Tables 1–4, §4.01 |

The rate schedules and standard deduction exist in the table because capping the
non-refundable portion of the credit at estimated tax liability is impossible without
them — and putting them in application code would violate the no-hardcoded-figures rule.

### Poverty guidelines — 2026

Source: **HHS ASPE**, <https://aspe.hhs.gov/topics/poverty-economic-mobility/poverty-guidelines>

48 contiguous states and DC. Sizes 1–8 are published directly ($15,960 through
$55,720); sizes 9–12 are generated by applying the published "add $5,680 for each
additional person" rule. **Alaska and Hawaii have separate, higher guidelines that are
not yet seeded** — families there currently get the contiguous-states figure, which
understates their poverty threshold. Worth fixing before either state is promoted.

### National cost ranges

Source: **Children's Bureau / Child Welfare Information Gateway**, *Planning for
Adoption: Knowing the Costs and Resources*, June 2022 —
<https://www.childwelfare.gov/resources/planning-adoption-knowing-costs-and-resources/>

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Adoption from the public child welfare system | "virtually free of cost" |
| Private agency adoption | $30,000 – $60,000 (all-inclusive) |
| Home study | $1,000 – $3,000 |
| Court documentation fees | $500 – $2,000 |
| Legal representation of adoptive parents | up to $4,500 |
| Intercountry adoption | $20,000 – $50,000 (all-inclusive) |
| Federal nonrecurring reimbursement ceiling | $2,000 |
| DoD military reimbursement | up to $2,000 per child / $5,000 per year |

**A deliberate limitation.** The Children's Bureau reports private-agency and
intercountry costs as single all-inclusive ranges that already cover the home study,
court and legal fees, birth-parent expenses, and post-placement supervision. Those
profiles are therefore seeded as **one line item each**, not decomposed — splitting an
aggregate into components would mean inventing the split. The result is a thin "show me
the math" drawer for unseeded states, which is the honest outcome and a direct
incentive to seed real state data.

This factsheet is from **2022**. It is the most recent national compilation the
Children's Bureau publishes, but the figures are four years old and should be treated
as the floor of a range, not the middle.

### USCIS filing fees

Source: **Form G-1055, Fee Schedule, edition 05/29/26** —
<https://www.uscis.gov/sites/default/files/document/forms/g-1055.pdf>

I-800A $920 · I-800 $920 · biometric services $30. (I-600A/I-600 carry the same $920
general filing fee and are not separately seeded; the Hague forms cover the majority of
current intercountry adoptions.)

### Florida

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Home study and adoptive-parent training | Free | [FL DCF Adoption Exchange — Benefits](https://adoptionexchange.myflfamilies.com/Benefits) |
| Agency / program fee | "little or nothing" | same |
| Circuit court adoption filing fee | $400 – $442 | Base fee capped at $395 by [Fla. Stat. 28.241(1)(a)1.a](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/28.241); clerks add charges — [Orange County](https://www.myorangeclerk.com/Divisions/Family/Family-Law) $400, [Nassau County](https://www.nassauclerk.com/206/Clerks-Fee-Schedule) $442 incl. sealing |
| Maintenance adoption subsidy | $5,000/year, paid monthly | [Fla. Stat. 409.166(4)(c)](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/409.166) |
| Nonrecurring expense reimbursement | up to $1,000 | Fla. Stat. 409.166(7) |
| Medicaid | to age 18, some to 21 | FL DCF Benefits |
| Postsecondary tuition exemption | to age 28 | Fla. Stat. 409.166(2)(a), FL DCF Benefits |
| Recognition of a foreign adoption decree | no Florida readoption required | [Fla. Stat. 63.192](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2026/63.192) |

Judgment calls worth knowing about:

- **Court filing is seeded as `$0 – $442`.** The highest county total found is $442;
  DCF says the agency can pay it for families who cannot afford it. The range spans
  both. (It was `$0 – $395` until October 2026, which understated what clerks charge.)
- **The monthly subsidy is stored as `41667` cents/month** — $5,000/year ÷ 12. The
  statute expresses it annually; the calculator's ongoing block is monthly throughout.
  It is also a negotiated figure ("or an amount other than $5,000 as agreed in
  writing"), so the seeded value is the statutory default, not a promise.
- **Intercountry:** the program fee and USCIS fees are not Florida's to set, so those
  rows repeat the national figures. The Florida row is finalizing or re-adopting in a
  Florida court, `$0 – $442` — required for an IR-4/IH-4 visa, optional otherwise.

### New York

| Figure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Home study and agency fee | None for a child in a local commissioner's guardianship | [OCFS adoption FAQ](https://ocfs.ny.gov/programs/adoption/faq.php) |
| Court filing fee | None — Family Court charges no filing fees | [NY Courts](https://www.nycourts.gov/node/60226) |
| Attorney to finalize | Generally required (OCFS); no state fee schedule, so national $0 – $4,500 | Children's Bureau |
| Maintenance adoption subsidy | $1,083.82 – $1,490.34/month basic rate (2026–27); special $2,338.46, exceptional $3,544.56, extraordinary $4,312.59 | [26-OCFS-ADM-10, Attachment A](https://ocfs.ny.gov/main/policies/external/2026/adm/26-OCFS-ADM-10.pdf) |
| Subsidy paid at 100% of board rate | Agreements approved since Sept. 1, 2023 | [18 NYCRR 421.24(a)(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/18-NYCRR-421.24) |
| Nonrecurring expense reimbursement | up to $2,000 | 18 NYCRR 421.24(d)(3) |
| Medicaid or medical subsidy | to 21 (disability), 18 (hard-to-place) | [OCFS adoption subsidies](https://ocfs.ny.gov/programs/adoption/post-adoption/subsidies.php) |
| Foster Youth College Success Initiative | Adopted from care after age 13 | [NYSED](https://www.nysed.gov/postsecondary-services/foster-youth-college-success-initiative) |

- **The monthly subsidy range is the basic rate only** — upstate age 0–5 to metro age
  12+. The higher tiers need a documented disability, so they are named in the detail
  text rather than widening the range, which would overstate it for most families.
- **The rates reset every July 1.** 26-OCFS-ADM-10 covers July 2026 – June 2027; the
  next ADM-10 supersedes it.
- **Intercountry:** as with Florida, only the court step is New York's own — finalizing,
  registering (Dom. Rel. Law 111-c), or re-adopting (Dom. Rel. Law 115-a), with no
  filing fee.

Florida and New York are seeded for **foster care and intercountry**, the only two
paths HavenLink offers. The engine still carries national rows for private domestic,
kinship, and stepparent adoption because the tests use them, but the API rejects those
types.

## Known gaps

1. **48 states + DC have no state-specific data.** They fall back to national ranges.
   This is designed behavior, not a bug — but the fallback banner appears on the large
   majority of results today.
2. **Alaska and Hawaii poverty guidelines** are wrong (see above).
3. **No state adoption tax credits are seeded.** `state_credit_rules_json` is empty, so
   families in states that offer one see only the federal credit — an understatement.
4. **The `calc_fpl` table is only lightly used.** It backs a household-income-versus-
   poverty-guideline readout for grant screening. No grant program eligibility is
   actually evaluated against it yet.
5. **International cost line items may slightly double-count.** The Children's Bureau
   $20,000–$50,000 range states that it covers "immigration processing", and the USCIS
   fees are itemized on top of it. The overlap is at most ~$1,870 against a $20,000+
   base, and the spec explicitly asks for immigration fees to be shown separately, so
   this is a deliberate trade in favour of itemization.

## Encryption at rest

The spec requires income data to be encrypted at rest. What the application does:

- **No exact income figure is ever collected, computed with, or stored.** Only a band
  id (`75k_100k`). This is the primary control.
- Saved estimates live in `calculator_results`, keyed to the owning user and readable
  only through endpoints scoped by `user_id`.
- Results are **never emailed** — unlike every other step in this codebase, which mails
  submissions to the shared team inbox. An agency must never see a family's income
  screen.

**Still to confirm:** that the RDS instance has `StorageEncrypted: true`. RDS is not in
`deploy/cloudformation/havenlink-ecs-fargate.yml` — it predates the template and was
created by the `deploy/scripts/*.sh` helpers — so this is a console/CLI check, not a
code change:

```bash
aws rds describe-db-instances --query "DBInstances[].{id:DBInstanceIdentifier,encrypted:StorageEncrypted}"
```

If it returns `false`, encryption cannot be enabled in place: it requires a snapshot,
an encrypted copy, and a restore. That is an infrastructure task, not an application
one.
